# Future Academic & Professional Aspirations – Shahrina Khan

## Vision & Direction
As I near the completion of my Bachelor of Science in Computer Science at UMT Sialkot, my focus is transitioning toward deeper specialization in full-stack architecture, agentic artificial intelligence, and applied language technologies.

---

## 1. Advancing Agentic AI & RAG Architectures
My work on **Arbitrix** and **FocusOS** demonstrated the vast potential of moving beyond simple single-turn prompts to autonomous, multi-agent reasoning loops:
- I aim to deepen my understanding of multi-agent frameworks, tool-use coordination, and deterministic guardrails.
- I am interested in building systems where language models don't just chat, but actively execute verifiable steps against databases, external APIs, and document stores with high precision and zero hallucinations.

## 2. Low-Resource Language NLP & Urdu AI
Building **SachAI** exposed the substantial resource gap that persists for Urdu and South Asian languages in modern natural language processing:
- I want to explore advanced tokenization strategies, instruction-tuning of open-weights models for Urdu, and multi-lingual evaluation benchmarks.
- Bringing accessible technology to Urdu-speaking users through seamless right-to-left (RTL) interfaces remains a personal passion.

## 3. Shipping Production-Grade Full-Stack Products
While academic and hackathon prototypes validate ideas, my ultimate satisfaction comes from shipping software that people genuinely use:
- I want to expand my skills in distributed systems, asynchronous event queues (e.g., Redis, Celery), CI/CD pipelines, and cloud containerization (Docker, Kubernetes).
- I strive to be a developer who bridges the gap between machine learning research and clean, reliable software engineering.

## 4. Contributing to the Developer Community
- I believe in open source and knowledge sharing.
- In the future, I plan to author technical tutorials, open-source reusable boilerplates for FastAPI and Next.js, and mentor incoming computer science students navigating their early semesters.
